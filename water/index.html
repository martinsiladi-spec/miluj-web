How much of you is water?

Almost all of you. Water molecules are so small and so many that about 99 of every 100 molecules in your body are water. Every other molecule you are made of lives, works and moves in it.

Same atoms. Different order.

Chemistry tells you what water is made of. Structure tells you how it is arranged, and arrangement can change everything.

Graphite: carbon in sliding sheets

In a pencil, carbon atoms form flat hexagonal sheets stacked loosely on top of each other. The sheets slide apart at a touch, which is why graphite is soft enough to write with.

Hardness 1–2 on the Mohs scale

Diamond: the same carbon, locked in 3-D

Rearrange exactly the same atoms into a rigid three-dimensional lattice, each one bonded to four neighbours, and carbon becomes the hardest natural material on Earth, clear and brilliant.

Hardness 10 on the Mohs scale

Ordinary water: a restless network

Liquid water is held together by hydrogen bonds that break and re-form trillions of times every second. Its order is fleeting, shaped by temperature, movement, minerals and the surfaces it touches.

Each hydrogen bond lasts about a trillionth of a second
EZ

Structured water: order at the boundary

Next to water-loving surfaces, such as the membranes and proteins inside our cells, water can line up in ordered, hexagonal layers. Researcher Gerald Pollack calls this the exclusion zone, or EZ water, and describes it as a fourth phase of water. Other scientists explain his observations differently; the question is open and actively studied.

Reported EZ layers: up to hundreds of microns thick

Order and movement

Water that gathers itself.

Clean water is only the beginning. Two researchers looked at how water orders itself: at the surfaces it touches, and in the way it moves.

At the boundary · Gerald Pollack

An ordered layer that holds charge.

Next to water-loving surfaces, such as cell membranes and collagen, Pollack’s lab at the University of Washington observes a gel-like, ordered layer of water. He calls it EZ water, a fourth phase between liquid and ice.

  • It holds charge. The ordered zone is negative, the water beyond it positive, like a small battery.
  • It moves on its own. In water-loving tubes, water flowed with no pump at all (2013).
  • It feeds on light. Infrared light makes the ordered zone grow.

In motion · Viktor Schauberger

Water that moves, lives.

The Austrian forester (1885–1958) spent his life watching wild mountain streams. Healthy water, he saw, rarely runs straight: it spirals, cools and gathers itself in vortices.

Water forced through straight pipes he called “dead”, water allowed to swirl and breathe “living”. MILUJ prepares its water through vortex movement in this spirit, and sets out to measure what that changes.

How to read this. These are observations and interpretations, and they are debated. Other researchers explain Pollack’s experiments with known physics such as diffusiophoresis, and “living water” is Schauberger’s view, not an established scientific term. MILUJ builds on both openly, and tests them.

Into every part of you

Water passes into the blood mainly through the small intestine. It shows up there within five minutes and, within about two hours, has spread evenly through all the water in your body.

Established physiology
03

Rivers inside you

Fascia wraps every muscle and organ in one continuous web. Fluid runs between its layers, and water bound in hyaluronan lets them glide. When the tissue dries out, the layers stick and movement stiffens.

Emerging research
04

Into the cell

Water enters each cell through aquaporins, protein channels that each let through about a billion water molecules a second. H₂ needs no channel: it is small enough to pass straight through the membrane.

Established physiology · Nobel Prize 2003
05

Where energy is made

Mitochondria turn food and oxygen into energy and leak a few aggressive radicals as they work. In a 2007 study H₂ neutralised the most aggressive one, the hydroxyl radical, and both became water. How much this helps people is still being studied.

Three lenses. One honest language.

Established physiology

Water is the medium for every process inside and between our cells, and normal hydration is essential to human function. Molecular structure changes the properties of matter, as carbon shows.

Emerging research

Molecular hydrogen is being studied for effects on oxidative signalling and cellular processes; clinical evidence is still developing. Ordered water near surfaces (EZ water) is reported and debated.

MILUJ exploration

Living, structured water in Schauberger’s sense, and hydrogen held stable for hours, are what MILUJ sets out to document, measure and have validated independently.
